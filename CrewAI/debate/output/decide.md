The **opposition to the motion** is more convincing.

### Why
The side against banning reservations makes the stronger case because it directly addresses the difference between **formal equality** and **real equality**. Their argument is that people in India do not begin from the same starting point due to long-standing caste discrimination, poverty, and social exclusion. That point is persuasive because simply removing reservations would not erase the unequal conditions that already exist.

### Strongest points from the opposition
- **Historical injustice still has present effects:** They argue that discrimination did not end in the past, so the consequences still shape access to education and jobs today.
- **Equality of opportunity is not enough when starting points are unequal:** The race analogy is effective in showing that treating unequal groups the same can still be unfair.
- **Reservations help prevent privilege from dominating completely:** They point out that wealth, coaching, connections, and family background already give some groups major advantages, so banning reservations could strengthen inequality rather than reduce it.
- **They offer reform, not rejection:** Instead of denying the system has flaws, they suggest improving it and combining it with better education and anti-poverty measures. That makes their position more balanced and practical.

### Weaknesses in the pro-motion side
The support for banning reservations is built mainly on the ideal of **equal treatment**, which is a noble principle, but it does not fully respond to the reality of unequal social conditions. The argument that reservations can reinforce caste identity is valid, but it is less convincing than the opposition’s claim that reservations still serve an important corrective purpose. Also, the pro side proposes alternatives like scholarships and free education, but these are not shown to be sufficient replacements for the protection reservations currently provide.

### Conclusion
While the motion appeals to the idea of a caste-free and equal India, the **anti-motion side is more convincing** because it better accounts for real-world inequality and shows why removing reservations now could deepen unfairness rather than solve it. Therefore, **reservations should not be banned at this stage; reform is more persuasive than removal**.
