I strongly support the motion that reservations should be banned in India because true equality cannot exist when opportunity is decided by caste, not merit, need, or ability.

Reservations were introduced as a temporary tool to correct historic injustice. But today, in many cases, they have become a permanent political system that divides people instead of uniting them. A child born today should not be punished or rewarded for what their ancestors were. Equality means every Indian should compete on the same platform, with the same rules.

Think about a hardworking student from a poor family who studies day and night but loses a seat to someone with lower marks only because of category benefits. That is not equality. That is replacing one unfairness with another. Real justice should help the poor, the struggling, and the deserving — regardless of caste.

Also, reservations deepen caste identity. Instead of removing the idea of “upper” and “lower,” they keep reminding people of it every single day in school, college, and jobs. If we truly want a modern India, we should build a society where a doctor is valued for skill, an engineer for talent, and a student for effort — not for birth.

There are many ways to support disadvantaged people without caste-based reservations: free quality education, scholarships for the poor, skill training, better schools in rural areas, and financial support based on income. These are fairer solutions because they uplift those who actually need help.

A united country cannot be built on division. India should move from caste-based reservation to need-based opportunity. If we want real equality, then everyone must be treated equally — no special privilege, no inherited disadvantage, only equal rights and equal chances for all.
